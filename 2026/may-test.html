The Monthly High-Five

The moves that matter from May 2026.

M&A
QXO Releases TopBuild Investor Q&A — Frames "Own the Job Site" Integration Strategy
On May 11, QXO released its investor Q&A outlining the strategic rationale for its pending $17B TopBuild acquisition. Brad Jacobs framed the deal as a move to "own the job site" — leveraging TopBuild's roughly 22,000 daily job-site visits to cross-sell, optimize inventory, and expand bundled offerings. QXO targets mid- to high-single-digit organic growth post-close. TopBuild released Q1 2026 financials on May 5 but cancelled its earnings call, reflecting transition-period protocols. Meanwhile, Tecta America — the nation's largest commercial roofing contractor, valued at $3–4B per Bloomberg sources — continues to explore strategic options with Altas Partners and Leonard Green & Partners having appointed advisers. The commercial roofing exit cycle is now formally underway.
⚡ The distribution layer has been claimed by QXO. The commercial contractor layer is now in motion with Tecta. The institutional capital that built the trades roll-up thesis is beginning its exit sequence.

Materials
June Manufacturer Price Increases Announced — GAF Cites "Sustained Surge" in Raw Material Costs
GAF's June 2026 distributor letter cites "sustained surge in key raw material costs" as the driver for a new round of increases, with CertainTeed issuing a separate freight surcharge increase effective May 11. The compounding effect: tariffs on MDI adhesive components (60%), TCPP flame retardants (272.7%), Section 232 steel/aluminum (25%), and petroleum-based asphalt remain fully in force. National average installed project cost on a 2,000 sq ft home has reached $20,500–$21,500. Construction material prices are 43.4% above February 2020 levels per NRCA data. Every bid submitted before May absorbs the gap on installation in June.
⚡ The tariff environment is broader and more structural than prior cycles. MDI and TCPP are not getting exemptions — they're domestic manufacturers' raw inputs from global supply chains. This isn't a temporary spike; it's a permanent cost floor reset.
3
Insurance
Verisk 2026 U.S. Roof Report: Severity Rising Even as Claims Volume Falls — 38% of U.S. Roofs in Poor Condition
Verisk's May 29 report is the most significant insurance-roofing data release of the year. Despite a near 20% decline in overall 2025 claims volume, average residential replacement cost reached $17,631 — a 33% jump vs. the prior 4-year average. Repair costs hit $4,699, up 25%. The culprit: sub-catastrophe hail events accumulating damage on an aging roof stock — 38% of U.S. roofs now rated moderate to poor condition, which Verisk data shows carry 60% higher loss costs than well-maintained roofs. In hail states, 57% of properties have roofs 9 years old or newer vs. just 38% in non-hail states, highlighting faster replacement cycles in storm corridors. The Midwest and Northeast carry the oldest roof inventory: 17–18% of roofs are 31+ years old.
⚡ For supplement-heavy operators in the hail corridor, this report is tailwind. For carriers, it's a pricing signal. Operators who document roof condition thoroughly at inspection are building the evidentiary foundation for supplement recovery before the adjuster even shows up.

PE Activity
Founders Advisors Releases May Roofing M&A Update — Residential Platforms Command 5–7x EBITDA; Commercial at Premium
Founders Advisors' May 13 roofing M&A update confirms persistent buyer appetite despite macro headwinds: residential roofing platforms are transacting at 4–7x EBITDA for bolt-ons, with PE-backed strategic buyers competing against standalone sponsors and corporate acquirers. Per industry tracking, 56+ PE-backed contractor platforms remain active, with Confluence Point (EXP), Royalty Roofing USA, Ridgeline, Vertex Service Partners, and Omnia Exterior Solutions (CCMP) all continuing bolt-on cadence. KPMG's 2026 Roofing Contracting report confirmed 2025 transaction volume reached all-time highs. Axial's live platform shows 92 companies currently marketed, 16 buyers competing per deal, and 1,970 investors actively seeking roofing acquisitions.
⚡ The question is no longer volume — it's quality. With 92 companies actively marketed, the buyer's diligence bar has risen. Gain/fade, supplement recovery %, and IC documentation are now table stakes in Q&A, not optional disclosures.
5
Regulatory
DOL Independent Contractor Final Rule Expected H2 2026 — State ABC Tests Remain the Operative Risk
Following the close of the 60-day comment period on April 28, the DOL's NPRM to rescind the 2024 independent contractor rule and restore a modified 2021 two-factor framework (control + opportunity for profit/loss) moves toward finalization. The SBA Office of Advocacy estimated the rescission saves small businesses approximately $2.31B over 10 years. Critically: the 2024 rule technically remains operative for private FLSA litigation, and state ABC tests in California, New Jersey, and Illinois are unaffected by federal rulemaking. Post-Loper Bright, courts are not bound by DOL deference. PE diligence teams are now requesting IC classification audits as a standard Q&A line item alongside gain/fade and supplement recovery.
⚡ Federal direction is favorable but not dispositive. If your platform has material revenue in CA, NJ, or IL and is using 1099 labor, the federal rule change doesn't protect you. The audit window is open — use it.
Retrospective

Lessons from the Ladder

History this industry learned from — or repeats.

The Supplement Suppression Era (2018–2022): When Carriers Won by Attrition

Through the late 2010s and into the pandemic era, a number of large national carriers systematically tightened supplement approval timelines, introduced proprietary scope tools, and trained field adjusters to challenge line items that had previously been accepted as standard. Contractors who lacked documentation discipline — no photos at pre-inspection, no Xactimate line-item justification, no public adjuster relationships — saw supplement recovery rates fall to the 30–40% range, leaving material dollars on the table on nearly every insurance job. The operators that survived and thrived built a supplement desk as a dedicated function, not an afterthought. By 2023–2024, supplement recovery became a competitive differentiator, not just an operational nicety.

🎓 Supplement recovery is margin recovery. In a high-severity, rising-cost environment, leaving 20 cents of every insurance dollar unfiled is the equivalent of bidding your retail jobs at a 20-point discount.

The 2008–2010 Housing Crash: What the Trades Roll-Up Survivors Had in Common

The first wave of home services consolidation hit a wall between 2008 and 2010 as new construction collapsed and insurance restoration was not yet institutionalized as a revenue base. Platforms with heavy new-construction exposure saw EBITDA fall 40–60% in 18 months; those with re-roof and maintenance-driven recurring revenue contracted only modestly. The PE sponsors who recouped capital were those invested in platforms with diversified revenue streams — re-roof, maintenance contracts, and commercial service — independent of the housing transaction cycle. Storm revenue and new-construction revenue are cyclical. Maintenance and re-roof are not.

🎓 The platform that can't articulate its non-cyclical revenue base in a down market will lose its multiple before it loses its customers. Underwrite the trough, not the peak.

What it measures: The ratio of supplement dollars actually paid by the carrier (or collected from the homeowner via negotiated settlement) versus the total dollars filed in supplement requests. Calculated at the job level and rolled to a trailing-twelve portfolio view. A job where $4,200 in supplements were filed and $2,940 was collected is a 70% recovery rate. Tracked per adjuster, per carrier, and per market, it reveals where the documentation is weak and where the carrier relationships need investment.

Why PE buyers care: In an insurance-restoration-heavy platform, supplement recovery % is often the single largest addressable margin opportunity in the business. The Verisk 2026 U.S. Roof Report confirmed that replacement severity is up 33% — but carriers don't automatically write those costs into initial estimates. The delta between Verisk-benchmarked replacement costs and what an average carrier writes in the first pass can be $2,000–$5,000 per job. A platform doing 1,000 insurance jobs per year at a 50% vs. 75% recovery rate on a $3,000 average supplement opportunity is leaving $750,000 in annual EBITDA on the table. At a 7x multiple, that's $5.25M in enterprise value not captured — before diligence even opens.

May 2026 relevance: The Verisk report, rising materials costs (asphalt shingle inputs up 7.1% in May), and a below-average storm forecast combine to create a specific pressure point: fewer total claims but higher individual claim values, in a rising-cost environment where initial adjuster estimates increasingly lag true replacement cost. Operators with supplement desks running documented Xactimate line-item workflows and photo-linked inspection reports are capturing the gap. Operators handling supplements reactively are subsidizing the difference out of job margin.

Watch-out: Supplement recovery % can be inflated by filing overly aggressive initial supplements that get partially paid — a 90% recovery rate on a $500 supplement is less valuable than a 75% rate on a $4,000 supplement. Pair this metric with average supplement filed per job and total supplement revenue as a % of insurance revenue to ensure the denominator is meaningful, not manufactured.

May Tech Spotlight

Things To Watch: HOVER

Each month we spotlight one tool, platform, or technology worth understanding — whether you're an operator, a PE investor evaluating platforms, or both.

If aerial measurement reports have been your estimating foundation, HOVER is the platform that's quietly changed the competitive calculus in 2026. HOVER is positioning itself not as a measurement vendor but as an end-to-end connected platform for the full renovation workflow — a significant repositioning from its origins as a smartphone-photo-to-3D-model tool. The January 13, 2026 Connected Platform launch is the pivot that matters: HOVER now unifies measurement, automated estimating, homeowner-ready proposals, and e-signature in a single solution powered by Hover AI and a spatial database of over 10 million residential properties, replacing up to five separate tools in an operator's stack.

What makes HOVER relevant in May 2026 is where it wins that no aerial provider can replicate: new construction and heavily-treed lots. EagleView and Nearmap are imagery-dependent — if the satellite pass hasn't captured the property, or dense tree cover obscures the roof plane, the measurement is either unavailable or degraded. HOVER requires only smartphone photos taken on-site, then generates a 3D model in hours. The January Connected Platform update added 1,000+ workflow integrations (including JobNimbus, AccuLynx, ABC Supply, Beacon Pro, CompanyCam, and Xactimate-compatible ESX export), making HOVER's output plug directly into the production and insurance workflows that storm-restoration operators already run. The pricing model is per-project (pay-as-you-go), meaning there's no monthly commitment risk — a significant advantage for platforms evaluating technology spend against volatile storm-driven volume.

The watch-out is per-scan economics at scale. At high volume, per-project pricing compounds: a platform running 500 HOVER scans per month at $25–$50 per scan is spending $12,500–$25,000 monthly before any other software cost. For platforms where aerial imagery covers the majority of service geography, the right stack is HOVER as a complement — for new construction, tree-coverage fallback, and insurance interior documentation — not as the primary measurement source. The diligence question on a target running HOVER exclusively isn't "what does it cost" but "what percentage of their jobs require on-site scanning vs. aerial, and is the per-scan cost embedded in job margin or absorbed as overhead?"

The Square Foot Bottom Line — May 2026

May 2026 confirmed what April's price increases telegraphed: the roofing industry is navigating a structural cost reset, not a cyclical spike. GAF's June distributor letter citing "sustained surge in raw material costs" and CertainTeed's May 11 freight surcharge represent the second consecutive month of manufacturer increases landing on top of still-active Section 232 steel/aluminum and Section 301 tariffs. MDI adhesive components face 60% anti-dumping duties; TCPP flame retardants face 272.7% duties. These are inputs to domestically manufactured shingles — not finished imports — which means IEEPA refund pathways offer no relief here. The national average installed project has moved to $20,500–$21,500. The cost floor has reset.

The most significant data release of the month was Verisk's May 29 U.S. Roof Report: average residential replacement cost reached $17,631 — a 33% jump over the prior four-year average — despite a 20% decline in 2025 claims volume. Severity is rising faster than frequency is falling, which means insurance-restoration operators are working fewer jobs at higher average ticket values against a carrier community that is increasingly scrutinizing supplement line items. The 38% of U.S. roofs in moderate-to-poor condition represent both an opportunity (demand pipeline) and a diligence risk (aging stock in PE-platform service areas may carry deferred maintenance that surprises on scope and cost). Supplement recovery % is the margin lever hiding in plain sight on every insurance-heavy platform.

On the M&A front, QXO's May 11 investor Q&A put the integration thesis on the record: "own the job site" through cross-sell and bundled offerings, targeting mid-to-high-single-digit organic growth. The TopBuild deal is pending shareholder vote. Simultaneously, Tecta America's sale process — with a $3–4B valuation floated by Bloomberg — signals that the PE-backed commercial roofing exit cycle is now formally open. The operators best positioned entering June are those who spent May doing three things: building or auditing their supplement desk workflow before the next storm event, tracking materials cost at the job level to capture the May/June manufacturer increase gap, and getting IC documentation current ahead of anticipated DOL final rule publication later in 2026. Everyone else is reacting to a market that has already moved.

Riddle Answer

Answer: Gap. In roofing, the gap is the opening between a roof surface and its flashing, cap, or termination detail — leave it open and water infiltrates the assembly at the weakest point, compounding damage far beyond the original defect. In private equity and insurance restoration, the gap is the delta between what was filed (or projected) and what was actually collected — leave it untracked and margin leaks out of every insurance job, compounding across a portfolio until a diligence team surfaces it as a structural discount to enterprise value. On a roof and on a balance sheet, the gap never closes itself. It has to be found, measured, and deliberately sealed.
